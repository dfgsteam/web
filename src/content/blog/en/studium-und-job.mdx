## The balancing act

Studying Applied Computer Science, Physics and Business at the same time while
developing projects on the side sounds like a lot. It is. But it is less a
question of talent than of structure.

## What works for me

- **Fixed blocks:** each subject gets fixed time slots in the week – not
  "sometime", but scheduled
- **One thing at a time:** real focus instead of task switching; the switch
  costs more time than it saves
- **Small, measurable steps:** one document, one chapter, one feature – clearly
  finished instead of forever open
- **Note it digitally:** one central list for everything, so nothing is managed
  in the head

## Why the mix still makes sense

The effort pays back: the subjects fuel each other. The mathematics from
physics helps with algorithms, the business perspective helps with client
projects, and the programming craft makes theoretical concepts tangible. Those
who connect both worlds see problems others don't.

## The honest core

There are weeks when it doesn't work. Then the answer is: name priorities,
reshape the calendar and accept that not everything has to be perfect.
Consistency beats perfection.
